Organics 101: Feed the Green, Not the Landfill
Organics 101: Feed the Green, Not the Landfill
Release Date: 2026-10-30
Thank you for participating in the City’s Organics Collection program by using your organics (green) cart and helping make Long Beach more sustainable!
Why did the City start an organics program?
It’s state law. California’s Senate Bill 1383 (SB 1383) requires residents and businesses to reduce organic material sent to the landfill.
It keeps organics out of the landfill. When food scraps and yard trimmings break down in a landfill, they release methane, a harmful gas that traps the sun’s heat, contributes to climate change, and can harm our health. Turning organics into compost helps cut pollution and protects our community.
Who is my hauler?
The City of Long Beach picks up trash and organics from all single-family homes and some businesses and apartment buildings. The City contracts with WM to pick up recycling. The City also has a Non-Exclusive Franchise system for other businesses and apartment buildings, as well as construction and demolition debris. Your hauler’s name will be printed on your trash, recycling, and organics carts or bins. You can find a list of permitted haulers in Long Beach and learn how to contact them on the City’s Private Waste Haulers page.
If you want to self-haul organics as a business, including landscaping businesses, you must comply with the self-haul requirements of Section 8.60.370.G of the Municode .
What can go in the organics (green) cart?
Items
Tips
Food Scraps
Examples Include:
Meat, eggs, bones, and shells
Coffee grounds
Bread and pasta
Cheese
Expired or spoiled food without packaging
Vegetables and fruits
Food-soiled paper (napkins, paper towels, pizza boxes)
Collect food scraps in a container or paper bag near food prep areas. After emptying containers into the green cart, clean and return them to prep areas.
Store collection containers in the freezer until collection day to help prevent odors.
Wrap food scraps in used paper.
Line the bottom of the green cart with cardboard or used paper to absorb liquids.
Yard trimmings
Examples Include:
Plant trimmings
Grass clippings
Leaves, flowers, twigs, and branches
Palm fronds, cacti, and succulents SHOULD NOT BE DISPOSED OF IN GREEN CARTS.
For palm fronds and large logs, request a special collection. Composting facilities do not accept these.
Remove non-plant material, such as nursery tags, pots, and plastic, and dispose of it in the trash.
Place yard trimmings in green carts unbagged.
Items that CANNOT go in the green cart:
Products labeled compostable or biodegradable
Food packaging
Palm fronds, cacti, or succulents
Logs and tree stumps
Hazardous wood waste or treated wood, such as furniture parts
Recyclables, including items listed on the Recycling Guidelines
Trash, including items listed on the Refuse Guidelines
Oil
Household Hazardous Waste (HHW) and electronic waste (e-waste). View more information on drop-off locations .
Bulky or oversized items and tires
Carpet
Non-compostable paper
Where does the organic material go?
Organic material from the green cart first goes to a transfer station. It is then sent to one of three industrial composting facilities: Recology Blossom Valley Organics , Sage Ranch Compost , or American Organics Composting Facility . Yard trimmings and food scraps are turned into compost, which helps improve soil quality.
By using your organics cart correctly, you help keep organic material out of landfills, cut methane pollution, and create healthy compost for our soil.
How Can I Reduce Waste?
In addition to participating in curbside recycling and organics collection service, there are many ways to reduce waste at home:
Shop smart and buy in bulk: Choose items with less packaging and make a list to avoid extra purchases. This can reduce waste and save you money!
Donate extra food: Visit the City’s Edible Food Recovery page to learn where to donate food.
Save and reuse containers: Save empty jars and reusable containers for leftovers, household items, small tools, or craft items.
Be creative with gifts and wrapping: Make a gift or give an experience to avoid waste. Wrap gifts with old newspapers, catalogs, or calendars.
Request paperless billing: Save paper by paying your monthly bills online.
Use rechargeable batteries: Save money and avoid waste by choosing rechargeable batteries over single-use ones.
Switch to reusables: Avoid single-use disposables like water bottles, straws, and utensils by bringing your own.
You can join a waste‑reduction workshop on the City’s Waste Reduction Workshops page.
You can also learn how to compost at home on the Composting at Home page or learn about community composting on the LB Community Compost website .
